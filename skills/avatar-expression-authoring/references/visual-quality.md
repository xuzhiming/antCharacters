# Judge the finish

Look at the sample and the working model under the same conditions. First look at the impression of the whole face, then at the areas with the largest differences. Do not stop at "natural" or "cute"; identify the lines and surfaces that differ from the target and connect them to the next edit.

| What to look at | Example of difference from the target | How to think about the next edit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closed-eye arc and outer eye corner | Too straight, the outer side drops sharply | Vary the movement at the center and both ends, and select the surroundings too to connect the arc. |
| Brows and the overall impression of the face | What should be a calm closed eye becomes a troubled or sad face | Overlay the neutral face, restore the brow position, tilt and curvature, and readjust the skin beneath. |
| Eyelid thickness and surface curvature | Puffy, floating forward, a groove below the brow | At profile and both 45-degree views, distribute the front-back position from the eyelid to the forehead smoothly into the surroundings. |
| Eyelashes and skin | Lines stretch, bands fold, gaps at the roots | Adjust the parts in order and distribute across several rows. Also check whether local pinning is stretching another area. |
| Mouth contour and expression | Only the corners rise, the center is stiff, the opening differs | Edit the upper and lower lip arcs, thickness, corners and cheeks together. |
| Likeness and intermediate values | Only maximum strength looks good, the impression differs from the neutral face | Compare at viewing size, go back to intermediate strength and with hair, and fix unintended expression changes. |

## From judgment back to editing

If you can write "the eye closes correctly, but the brows sit lower than the target and look sad. Restore the brow height and arc, and adjust below the brows," the next operation is clear. Do not leave a fixable difference visible in the comparison images as merely a description of remaining work.

Once the large differences are reduced, prioritize the impression at viewing size. If pointed eyelashes or painted detail also exist in the neutral face, do not assume they are transfer defects. Respect the original design and the user's tolerance; it is complete once a natural, cute expression is reached. Do not keep making additional revisions just because detail shows at close range.

When revision is needed, keep the attempts that improved things and revert the ones that made them worse. If the same operation does not make progress, change the viewpoint, selected points, influence range or parts, and if the cause is uncertain, check the structure. Do not make an automatic aesthetics score or a uniform image error the completion standard.

Even when there is no mouth interior to begin with, prioritize bringing the lip edges closer to the target. Do not weaken the opening just because the inside is visible, and do not lose the required mouth shape. Explain the constraints of the original model's mouth structure separately from whether the lip-edge modeling is good.

## When presenting

Show the contour, expression and cuteness that follow the target through comparisons. Separate structural impossibilities or generation variance on the sample side from shortcomings on the model side. Do not send an expression the user accepted back to awaiting revision based on the agent's own minute standards. Present the target / pre-revision / finished states and "what was made beautiful and how," rather than logs or test counts.
