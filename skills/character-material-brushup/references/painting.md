# One painting quality standard

The standard is the painting of a finished VTuber or Live2D character illustration seen up close, finished through to the inside of each form. Do not use different art styles for the head, hair and body; render the differences in appearance caused by material within the same expression. This standard is a production goal; it does not mean stable reproduction has been verified on any particular generative model.

Aim for a state where the color layering and material differences read at normal display size, and enlarging reveals finished detail down to local areas of the eye area, hair and clothing. Do not make the completion standard an asset that simply enlarges the source illustration's simplicity, or adjustments that can barely be told apart.

## Shared design of color and density

- **Large forms**: While preserving the base color, lightness, saturation and hue shift gently. Even white has layers of pale color, and even black has chromatic light areas that can be read. Do not make the whole image a uniform color cast.
- **Readable shape**: Place clear boundaries at key overlaps and folds, and connect the inside of forms with soft transitions. Do not settle for painting that blurs everything, nor for painting with only a base color and a single shadow tone.
- **Local finish**: Paint detail color and reflections appropriate to each location, such as eyelids, lips, hair sheen, collars, cuffs and hardware. Do not substitute adding fine lines or patterns for quality.
- **Open space**: Leave quiet areas on broad surfaces of skin and cloth. Do not put strong shading or light points into every pixel; create places where the eye gathers and places where it rests.
- **Relationship of line and paint**: Keep the contours of the face and objects readable, and where needed blend them into a dark color that suits the skin, hair or clothing. Do not thicken contours or change shapes in order to deepen the painting.

## Face and skin: color that continues from each location into the surroundings

Keep the skin's base tone and light areas, and layer warmth and soft shading suited to each location onto the upper eyelid, outer eye corner, lower eyelid rim, cheeks, nose tip, ears and mouth area. Vary the width and density of each, connecting the distinct color near the eyes to the soft color on the cheeks. Do not stop at "make the whole face pink" or "just add blush to the cheeks." Do not turn the original skin color white; match the hue and strength of the warmth to that character.

Do not place the eyelashes as a single flat black symbol; within the existing shapes you can paint layers of deep color and a fine colored sheen. Give the sclera the pale shading it needs and the lower eyelid a rim color and small sheen, so that skin, eyelashes, sclera and iris read as connected. Do not change the eyelash count or length, or the eye contour, on your own.

Keep the iris's existing colors, pupil and features, and paint color layers and organized reflections from the deep color at the top to the brightness at the bottom. For the lips, paint delicate color, soft modeling and the small gloss needed within the shape and expression specified and adopted for this asset, finishing around the mouth line as well. Do not revert an asset normalized to a neutral expression to the source illustration's smile or open mouth for the sake of painting. Do not fill gaps in the skin or mouth area with iris detail alone.

Stars, hearts, unusual pupils, freckles, diagonal cheek lines and glitter are design features specific to a character. Do not add them to every character even if they appear in references. Keep painting sheen separate from multiplying light points and ornaments. Do not darken nose or cheek shadows to change the character's age or bone structure.

On exposed skin such as the neck, collarbones, arms, hands and knees, also paint skin color that connects to the face, with warmth and modeling suited to each location. Do not leave a state where only the face is finished and the body's skin is a flat single color.

## Hair: finish the surfaces of the existing locks

Preserve the hairstyle, length, parting, the shapes and placement of the locks, and the ornaments. Create smooth color transitions inside each lock, and combine depth at the roots and overlaps with broad soft sheen and a limited number of sharp reflections. Organize the hues of the light, midtone and dark areas, and paint depth without losing the hair's base color.

Do not use methods such as putting the same white band on every lock, making the hair glow all over, or filling in with fine hair lines or added locks. Paint sheen at a size where the existing locks remain readable, and leave color in the dark hair at the back as well. Do not make white hair a single flat white, or black hair a black mass.

## Outfit: vary reflections and shading by material

Preserve the construction, patterns, seams, hardware, body type and pose, and paint each material through to its surfaces and details.

| Material | Colors and texture to paint |
|---|---|
| Matte cloth and knit | Broad soft color variation, shading of existing folds and hems, thickness. Even with restrained reflections, do not make the surface a single color |
| Smooth cloth, satin, glossy outerwear | Modeling that follows the surface curvature, soft reflection bands with localized sharp highlights, depth at overlaps |
| Leather, shoes, hard accessories | Organized sheen that follows the surface direction, shading at edges, joints and existing panels |
| Metal and gemstones | Narrow light areas and tight dark areas, reflection of surrounding colors. Do not use the same blurring as cloth |

Specify only the materials that exist on the target. Do not turn the whole outfit into satin or plastic, and do not transplant lavish ornaments from references. You may add painted shading that follows the shape, but do not increase wrinkles, seams, hardware or pattern structures on your own to gain density.

## Organizing the lighting

What to remove: strong color casts from the original background, cast shadows on the floor, backlighting and glow effects, and unwanted shadows left over from removed parts. On a head where face and hair are a single unit, do not uniformly erase even the shading that shows the overlap between the retained hair and the face.

What to keep or add: local skin color, gradients within a form, modeling that reads the volume, and painted sheen that shows the material. Even when a new dramatic light source is unnecessary, keep the modeling a finished painting needs. Do not make the shared instructions `flat colors`, `single shadow tone`, `no highlights` or `minimal shading`, and do not let `neutral lighting` alone limit the finish. Maintain the same materials and color design across directions.

## Output review

Look at both the whole and the enlarged view. In addition to the file check, verify the following for each material on the target.

- Are the original facial features, locks and outfit construction preserved, and has the added detail avoided becoming new shapes or patterns?
- Do the large forms have color variation, with clear boundaries and soft transitions coexisting at key points?
- Is the face finished through the eyelids, eyelashes, cheeks and mouth area, the hair through the surface of each lock, and the clothing through its materials and edges?
- Are you avoiding treating as improvement only adding warmth, only deepening shadows, only raising saturation, or only adding light points?
- Is only one part rendered while others remain flat? Do the material differences and quiet open areas remain?

If it fails, name the location and the missing expression, such as "the color of the lower eyelid breaks off from the surrounding skin," "the inside of the lock is a single color," or "cloth and hardware have the same reflection," and revise that range.
