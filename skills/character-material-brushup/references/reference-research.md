# Searching for finished works and verbalizing them for generation

## What to look for

For new production, visit Pinterest and look for finished VTuber or Live2D character illustrations, finished close-ups of faces and hair, and finished outfit illustrations. Choose the look of finished work first, and use how-to explanations only as an aid to understanding the expression you need. Do not treat search-result titles or captions as having been observed; actually open and look at the images.

The purpose is to make one painting standard concrete. Do not mix characters or genres to create a new design, and do not use an artist's name as an art-style specification. For the head and body of the same request, use a shared approach to line, color layers, shading and sheen.

If there is an adopted sample of your own, prefer that sample and its corresponding verbalization. Use Pinterest observation to understand the standard and to make missing materials concrete, and do not replace the standard with a different art style on each search. If there is no sample of your own yet, use the [shared painting standard](painting.md), and do not write that you referenced a sample that does not exist.

## Starting points for searching

Start with queries such as `VTuber model art close up`, `Live2D model illustration face closeup`, `VTuber illustration face close up`, `VTuber full body illustration outfit` and `Live2D character art hair closeup` that specify **the finished work and the part you want to see**. If needed, you can also try other languages such as `버튜버 일러스트` or `虚拟主播 立绘`. The phrases are examples; actually check whether the results contain the works you want.

Add terms such as `white jacket`, `dark hair` or `fabric` depending on the asset. Do not shift the focus of the search to `anime shading tutorial`, `how to draw`, `beginner` or `step by step`. If `cloth shading` alone yields mostly explanations, return the search to finished outfit illustrations. If needed, use the search engine's `site:pinterest.com/pin/` or image search as an entry point and proceed to viewing finished images.

## Selection and observation

Do not select works based only on "cute," "high detail," "popular" or "heavily ornamented." Choose works where the needed parts of the face, hair and clothing can be seen well enough to read the color layers, hard and soft boundaries, material differences and open space of the [painting standard](painting.md). Do not take as representative of finished quality an image whose appeal comes from background effects, or a process diagram with only a base color and a single shadow tone.

Summarize each observation as "location → visible color, boundary and reflection → how to use it this time." Examples:

- A fine warm tone on the upper eyelid, a slightly denser color on the lower eyelid rim, and a broad pale color continuing onto the cheek → match this to the current skin color and connect from around the eyes to the cheek, varying density and width.
- Broad soft sheen in the center of a lock, tight chromatic color at the overlaps, fine reflections only at key points → paint sheen of varied size and depth inside the existing locks.
- Gentle color variation across the jacket surface, clear folds, narrow highlights on the hardware → do not give cloth and metal the same gloss; specify the reflection of each.

Separate shape, color scheme, outfit motifs, iris symbols, freckles, diagonal cheek lines and the like as designs specific to that reference work. Adopt only the expression this request needs, and do not turn observations into instructions to add everything.

## Pass only text to generation

For third-party reference images, including Pinterest ones, do not use the source image, crops, screenshots, image URLs or embedded data as direct references for image generation. The only thing passed into the generation prompt is words extracted from observation. Clearly separate usable production inputs such as the source illustration from observation-only images, and avoid instructions that pull in all recent images at once.

Do not save or copy third-party images into the kit, skill, ZIP or distributed files. Do not make an artist's name or a particular work's color scheme a fixed rule; reuse only the generalized relationships of painting. When you cite an observation source, show it as a link in the conversation, and do not add image copies or a source-list file to the delivery.

If you cannot view an image due to a login restriction or similar, do not pretend you saw it. Switch to public images or to what can be confirmed on the artist's public page. If you still cannot view it, briefly state the limitation, use the existing verbalized standard, and do not substitute search-result text for image observation.

## A shared sample of your own

If the user later designates a sample of their own to bundle with the kit, use it to unify the painting standard for head, hair and outfit. Do not infer permission to use or bundle from the mere presence of a file. Do not treat a third-party image provided this time as having become a sample of your own.

For a request to create a sample of your own with Midjourney or similar, specify the painting above in text only, and do not reproduce a particular work's character or clothing. Do not narrow it to a face-only standard; choose a composition that can show the same level of finish for face, hair and outfit. Add model-specific parameters only after checking the environment in use. Produce, adopt and bundle samples only within the explicitly requested scope, and do not add them automatically during normal asset production.
